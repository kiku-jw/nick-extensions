# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People who read and study public JW.org material in desktop Chromium browsers, plus future Safari iPhone/iPad and Firefox Android users, and want a few repetitive actions to take less time while keeping personal study data local.

## Product Purpose

Provide clear, optional helpers directly on JW.org and WOL pages without replacing the site or sending personal activity to another service. Success means users can immediately see what is available on the current page and complete focused tasks such as copying a paragraph, keeping a local note, or downloading selected Bible verses as audio.

## Brand Personality

Clear, calm, trustworthy. The extension should feel like a restrained browser utility, not a separate product layered over JW.org.

## Anti-references

- Dense technical feature dumps with no primary workflow.
- Important controls that exist only as undiscoverable hover behavior.
- Decorative dashboards, intrusive floating chrome, or interfaces that compete with the source page.
- Naming or styling that could imply official affiliation with JW.org.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the current-page task and one plain-language instruction.
2. Reuse JW.org's own selections and page structure instead of inventing parallel navigation.
3. Keep advanced controls available through progressive disclosure.
4. Process user-requested media locally and fetch only official public media sources.
5. Make every injected surface removable, keyboard-accessible, and scoped to supported pages.
6. Keep one deliberately smaller mobile feature set across Safari and Firefox. Treat it as unavailable to ordinary users until a signed provider link is live and verified. A clearly labeled TestFlight or signed Firefox beta may supply physical-device evidence; general release still requires the promised workflows to pass on real devices.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA contrast, visible keyboard focus, semantic controls, touch-accessible actions, and reduced-motion behavior. Status and errors must be understandable without relying on color alone.
